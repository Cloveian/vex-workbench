# Using it
how to actually build something with it, as it is right now

## Placing parts

Pick the VEX workbench and hit **VEX parts** to open the dock. The families are at the top (greyed out ones dont have a generator yet), vex's own parts are in the folders under them, and the search box filters both. Pick a family, set its options, then **Place** (or just double-click). If theres an active assembly (or part) it goes in there.

- **Wheels**, **Gears** and **Sprockets** up top are vex's own step parts picked the same way (type, then size, then kind or whatever), each box only has what goes with the ones above it, so you cant land on a wheel that doesnt exist. theyre the same parts as in the folders, and a placed one has the boxes in its properties wherever it came from, so a 4" omni can turn into a 3.25 traction right where it is (joints on its holes only come along if the new one has a hole in the same spot)
- the first time a variant gets placed it has to be built, a 35 long channel takes a few seconds (theres a wait cursor). after that its instant, every copy in that file links to the same source. a step part is slower the very first time, some take half a minute to read, then its kept as a brep in FreeCAD's cache folder (`~/.cache/FreeCAD/v1-1/Cache/VexWorkbench` on linux) and loads in a blink in every file after
- a placed part has its options in its properties, under VEX. change Holes or Size or whatever and *just that one* swaps to the other variant, the rest stay put
- vex's step parts keep vex's colors, generated ones get a color per material (the license plates by their color), and so do vex's steps with no color in them at all (nuts, LS shafts, retainers, couplers off their material, the rest off the community fusion library's appearances: red acetal gears and sprockets (the 12T Metal Gear stays steel), near black bearings, grey 9P chain, charcoal V5 sensors). the wheels get repainted in fusion's colors too, since most of their steps have no color and a couple are cad green and red: dark grey hubs with light grey rubber on the antistatic ones, grey rollers on a near black hub (2" omni) or a charcoal one (V1 2" mecanum), a grey hub on the 4" mecanum (fusion has no V2 2" mecanum, so its still one grey solid). anything with a material also gets FreeCAD's own material card for it (Steel-Generic, Aluminum-6061-T6, PA6 for nylon, POM for acetal, PTFE), so FEM and the Materials stuff see it. star screws get the head color vex's color coded pack has for that length, 0.290 yelow up to 2.500 brown (no 0.25 in the pack, so thats plain steel). the seam line down round generated stuff (screws, standoffs, spacers, washers, extra low nuts, HS and pitch shafts) is drawn in the part's own color so it doesnt show, the ends keep their lines so you can see where one spacer stops and the next starts

## Snapping

Click one or more holes, then **Frame on hole**. Each one gets a frame on its exact center, z out of the face you clicked and x along the length.

- clicking right on the edge of the hole is the sure way. on step parts with a lot of little detail (the motor is covered in vents) a click on the face *near* a hole can still grab a tiny loop next to it
- shafts have no hole, so on a shaft (and standoffs, spacers, washers) the frame goes on the axis at whichever end you clicked closer to
- screws: click the underside of the head, the shank is the hole there, so the frame lands where the head sits. nuts: the bore
- frames live in the part's source, so every copy of that part has them, and clicking the same hole again just gives the same frame back
- link arrays and linked sub-assemblies work too, click the hole on whichever copy you mean and it lands on that one

Then over in the Assembly workbench, joint two frames together (Fixed for bolted stuff). The joint picks whether the two z axes point the same way or opposite from how the parts sit when you make it, so if they come out *inside* each other instead of face to face, hit **Reverse** in the joint panel. either way the centers land exactly on each other.

## Hole dots

All the clicking tools (Align, Screw, Spacers, Bend, Cut, Drill) put an orange dot on every hole while their panel is open, on both sides of the metal, plus the ends of shafts and standoffs. on vex's step parts its only 8-32 sized holes a screw can get to from outside, plus the 1/8 and 1/4 shaft holes (gears, wheels, the 5.5W and 11W outputs), so the motors get their screw holes and not the vents. round holes on metal get them too, anything 0.1 to 0.5 across, drilled ones included, and a U-channel's inside ones sit just off the metal in the gap round the c-channel it goes over. theyre drawn on top of everything, so ones behind a part show and click too, and a click anywhere within about 14 px of one gets it (two dots on top of each other, like both sides of a hole, go to the one facing you). click a dot and its exactly that hole, a bigger green dot stays on it till the tool's done (face clicks get one on the hole they picked too). screws, nuts, spacers and washers dont get dots, and the dots all go away on Close. clicking a face still works too (nearest hole, except on Drill, where the click is the spot it drills). parts placed, moved or deleted while a panel is open get their dots sorted out a moment after.

## Align

**Align** does what Snapping does in two clicks, without any frames: click a hole on the part you want to move, then the hole on the part it goes against, and it jumps over so the holes line up and the faces sit flat on each other. it keeps the way the part was already turned, rounded to the angle box, so a fresh part comes out square to the other one. **Rotate -** / **Rotate +** turn the last one around the hole by the angle in the box (90 to start, anything works, + is counterclockwise looking at the face it went against), and **Gap** holds it that far off the other face (under 0 pushes it in, like a shaft end going through a hole), both work after its moved. with an assembly active it gets a Revolute joint between the two holes, so it can still spin, and the gap and turn show up as the joint's offset and rotation. lining the same thing up somewhere else swaps that joint for the new one, the old one would just drag it back. click a hole on one element of a link array and the whole array moves so that element's hole lands. tick **Move its whole group** and it moves the Part (or sub-assembly) the clicked part is in instead, the top one under the active assembly, so a whole drive side goes over in one go and everything in it stays where it was in it.

Tick **Slide along to another hole** for something that slides on a rail, like a linear slide or a lift carriage. after the two clicks it asks for one more hole on the same face of the rail, and the Revolute turns into a Slider from the first rail hole to that one, with its length limits on those two holes. it cant turn on the rail anymore, so Rotate does nothing after that. needs an active assembly, and then FreeCAD's own drag (double click the assembly, drag the part) slides it along to see it move.

## Screws

**Screw** opens a panel with the screw (Star or Shoulder) and the nut to use, it remembers both. click a hole on the head side, then the one on the nut side (the outside faces), and in goes the shortest screw that reaches all the way through the nut, nut and all. it stays open for the next one till you hit Close. with an assembly active they go in it, each with a Revolute joint to its hole, so it can spin on it.

Pick No nut, or click a threaded hole second (standoffs, and anything under 0.164 across like the motors), and the second click is the face the screw goes *into*. it aims for 3/8 of thread, takes 1/4 if the hole is shallower, and never goes past the bottom. Spacers in gaps fills every gap between the two clicked parts.

For a row of them, click a few holes on the head side first (all on one face, a second click on one unpicks it), then across. with **Auto finish** on, one click on the far face does every hole that has one lined up under it, off and its a click across for each. any without a hole across stay picked.

Tick **Pick the screw myself** and pick one from the list next to it, then its one click: the hole it goes in, head on that face, no nut and no spacers. Shoulder screws only go in this way, the shoulder length is your call.

**Spacers** does just the filling: click holes on two faces across from each other, fewest spacers + washers within 1/64 under the gap, never over. spacer OD is a setting, 0.375 to start.

## Bending, cutting, drilling

**Bend** folds any VEX metal part on a line through a hole: click the hole (same dots), and it bends there by the **Angle** (90 to start, + folds toward the face you clicked, - away from it). **Line** picks across the length or along it, **Off the hole** slides the line over (0.25 puts it between two rows), and **Other side turns** swaps which side moves, the shorter one moves to start. all of those redo the last bend till you click the next hole. **Inside radius** is 0.032 to start like vex's own bends (go down to 0 for a vise and hammer one), and **Bend sits** puts the bend next to the line or centered on it. at 0.032 a 90 eats about 0.08" of flat.

Only that one copy bends, the rest stay straight, and it still counts as the straight part in the parts list (thats what you buy). change Holes on it and it keeps its bends (the first bend, cut or hole that doesnt fit the new size gets dropped, and so does everything done after it, with a warning in the report view). channels bend too, flanges and all, which real metal wont do without cutting them first, so thats on you :P. only away from the flanges tho, folding the web into its own flanges gets refused (angles the same with their other leg)

**Cut** works on the same kind of line (Line, Off the hole), the smaller side goes and **Keep the other side** swaps it. across through a hole is cut to length, along a flange cuts just that flange (the far one stays). slide the line 0.25 over from a flange's hole row (one way or the other, whichever lands on the web) and the whole flange comes off flush, do both and the web can fold toward where the flanges were. sawn edge, no notches or chamfers. a cut that would leave it in two pieces gets refused.

Set Cut to **Two points** for a saw cut that doesnt have to go all the way across: click where it starts and where it ends, on the same face. holes, the grid (**Show the grid**) and dots along the outside edges all work, a face click snaps to the grid and Shift skips that. it takes out a **Kerf** wide strip (0.04 to start), an end on an edge runs off it, and anything it cuts loose goes, the biggest piece stays (Keep the other side for the smallest). so a slot is one cut in off the edge, a notch is three, and edge to edge is cut to length.

For anything fancier set it to **Sketch it** and click the face. a sketch starts right there (same dots and grid snap, x along the length), the panel closes and you're in the sketcher. draw whatever, and on Close everything closed in it gets cut through that wall, a shape inside another one is a hole in it, and an island it leaves goes like with two points. the sketch stays in the tree attached to that copy, so it moves with it. double-click it to change it and the cut redoes, delete it and the metal comes back. one with nothing closed in it doesnt cut anything.

**Drill** puts a round hole through the wall where you click, **Size** across (0.17 to start, 8-32 clearance). it snaps to the **Grid** (0.25 to start), laid out from the first hole on that face, so any size is one even grid, hold Shift to drill right where you clicked. tick **Show the grid** and every grid spot on the metal (or an LS gear) gets a dot too, so you can see where its going to land. a drilled hole gets a dot like any other. on a plate the grid spots between holes already have little square holes, so drilling one of those just opens it up. LS gears drill too, so they spin free on a screw: click the hub and it bores the square hole out round (0.18 or so clears it), straight through the whole gear. anything else that isnt metal gets refused.

Bends, cuts and drilled holes all go on that copy in the order you did them, and it still counts as the plain part. joints on its holes come along as long as the hole is still there (changing Holes too, on any part), and a hole on the side a bend turns takes its joint round with it, so a screw in it goes along. redoing the bend at another angle moves it again. the ones on a hole a cut took off break. a Frame on hole with nothing jointed to it yet comes along through the bends, cuts and holes, not a Holes change on a plain part. so cut before screwing things on. a joint made with freecad's own joint tool straight on a face, edge or corner comes along too, a face as long as any of it is left (drilled, cut shorter), an edge or corner only if its still right there or went round with a bend.

## Duplicate

**Duplicate** copies whatevers selected (parts, or a Part / sub-assembly full of them) right next to the originals, in the same group. **Mirror** puts the copy on the other side of a plane square to the X, Y or Z axis of the active part or assembly, **Plane at** that far along it, so a left drive side mirrored across YZ at 0 makes the right one. **Turn** turns the copy about that axis by the angle instead. a mirror never flips a part inside out: each one gets turned so it lands where its mirror image would, which for anything symmetric (channels, plates, gears, most of it) is exactly the mirror image. handed parts cant do that. the mecanum wheels swap to their other hand (Left <-> Right, Kind box and all) and land in the mirror spot, spot on for the V2s, close for the V1s since vex's two V1 models arent exact mirrors. anything else that doesnt mirror exactly (the motors, which have no other hand, and a few that only nearly mirror like the V2 HS gears and some wheels) gets turned into place across the plane it comes closest to mirroring on, so its shaft still points the mirrored way, and the panel lists it. bent, cut or drilled metal with no symmetry left (a bend and one off center hole) gets its steps mirrored, so the copy's bend and holes are the mirror ones. a cut out from **Sketch it** comes along mirrored too, but editing that sketch only recuts the original. the copies link the same sources, so they cost nothing and still count in the parts list. joints with both ends on copied parts come along (screws, nuts, Align's spin joints, slides), mirrored ones on the mirrored holes with the spin going the other way. on a part that only nearly mirrors (a motor, the V2 HS gears with their few odd bits) a joint comes along if theres a hole where its mirrored frame lands. a joint to something that wasnt copied (the chassis a drive side sits on) goes to the hole on it where the mirror or turn puts its frame, so a side mirrored across the middle of a symmetric chassis comes out bolted to the other side. if theres no hole there it stays behind and the panel says how many did, Align those again.

## Parts list

**Parts list** counts every placed VEX part by name into a spreadsheet in the document ("VEX parts list"), with the weight of each and the total. each is the plain part off the shelf, the total is what the placed ones really weigh, so a cut down copy counts lighter (a bent one a hair heavier). vex's step parts get vex's listed weight where there is one (the motors, brain, battery, most sensors, most wheels), otherwise one material for the whole thing off its folder, which is a guess and shows a ~ (so does the grand total if any of those are in it). inserts, a few wheels and anything else with neither show a ?, same as the license plates, and both get counted at the bottom. it counts every element of a link array and every copy of a linked sub-assembly too.
